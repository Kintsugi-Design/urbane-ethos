The Stigma Around Mental Illness and Its Impact on Men: A Scoping Review

2024-06-27 · 3 min read

“The stigma around mental illness profoundly shapes men’s experiences and creates significant barriers to their wellbeing and care. The authors call for more research to better understand the gendered nature of mental illness stigma and develop tailored interventions to address it.”

  • McKenzie et al. (2022)

Mental illness stigma significantly impacts men, shaping their experiences and creating barriers to their wellbeing and care. The stigma surrounding mental health challenges discourages men from seeking help, leading to detrimental outcomes. In support of Men’s Mental Health Month, this essay achieves to explore the main themes from a scoping review conducted by McKenzie et al. (2022) on how men experience stigma related to their mental health.

1. The Weight of Societal Stigma

One of the predominant themes identified is the profound impact of societal stigma. Many men reported feelings of shame, embarrassment, fear, and isolation due to negative stereotypes and stigmatizing attitudes from peers, family, healthcare providers, and the broader community. This self-stigma is a significant barrier, preventing them from seeking help and disclosing their struggles. The fear of being judged or misunderstood leads many men to internalize their mental health issues, which exacerbates their conditions and delays recovery.

2. Stigma in Male-Dominated Environments

Another critical theme is the stigma present in male-dominated environments such as workplaces and sports settings. Men in these areas often fear being perceived as weak or failing to uphold masculine ideals of strength and self-reliance. This fear leads them to hide their mental health struggles and avoid seeking support. The pressure to conform to traditional masculine norms makes it difficult for men to express vulnerability, which is essential for addressing mental health issues effectively.

3. Inequity-Driven Stigma

The review also highlights the intersection of mental illness stigma with other forms of marginalization, such as race, class, and sexual orientation. Men from marginalized groups face compounded stigma, which further disadvantages them. This intersectionality creates additional barriers to accessing mental health care and support. Understanding the layered nature of stigma is crucial for developing effective interventions that address the unique challenges faced by different groups of men.

4. De-Stigmatizing Strategies

Despite the pervasive stigma, the review identifies several promising strategies men use to manage or reduce its impact. Peer support is a powerful tool, providing a safe space for men to share their experiences and support each other. Improving mental health literacy helps men understand their conditions better and reduces misconceptions. Reframing help-seeking as a strength rather than a weakness is also essential in challenging traditional masculine norms and encouraging men to seek the help they need.

Conclusion

The stigma around mental illness profoundly affects men, shaping their experiences and creating barriers to their wellbeing and care. Understanding the gendered nature of mental illness stigma is crucial for developing tailored interventions that address these challenges. Further research is needed to explore these themes in-depth and devise effective strategies to combat stigma and support men’s mental health. By doing so, we can create a more inclusive and supportive environment for men struggling with mental health issues.

Reference: McKenzie, S. K., Oliffe, J. L., Black, A., & Collings, S. (2022). Men’s experiences of mental illness stigma across the lifespan: a scoping review. American journal of men’s health, 16(1), 15579883221074789.
